\section*{Chapter 3 Motion in a Straight Line}

\section*{3.1 Introduction}

Motion is a change in the position of an object with time. In this chapter we study motion along a straight line, where the position of the object can be given by a single coordinate $x$. We define displacement, velocity and acceleration and derive the equations of motion for uniform acceleration.

\section*{3.2 Equations of Motion}

For an object moving with uniform acceleration $a$, the velocity after time $t$ is $v=u+a t$ and the displacement is $s=u t+\frac{1}{2} a t^{2}$. Eliminating $t$ gives $v^{2}=u^{2}+2 a s$.

Example 1 A car starts from rest and accelerates uniformly at $2 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}^{2}$. Find the distance covered by the car in the first $5 \mathrm{~s}$.

Solution: Here $u=0, a=2 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}^{2}$ and $t=5 \mathrm{~s}$. Using $s=u t+\frac{1}{2} a t^{2}$ we get $s=\frac{1}{2} \times 2 \times 25=25 \mathrm{~m}$.

Example 2 A ball is dropped from the top of a tower $80 \mathrm{~m}$ high. Taking $g=10 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}^{2}$, find the time taken by the ball to reach the ground.

Solution: Using $s=\frac{1}{2} g t^{2}$, we get $80=5 t^{2}$, so $t=4 \mathrm{~s}$.

\section*{EXERCISE 3.1}

1. A body starts from rest with a uniform acceleration of $4 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}^{2}$. Its velocity after $3 \mathrm{~s}$ is
(a) $4 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$
(b) $8 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$
(c) $12 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$
(d) $16 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$
2. The slope of the position-time graph of a body moving along a straight line gives its
(a) acceleration
(b) velocity
(c) displacement
(d) distance
3. A stone is thrown vertically upward with a speed of $30 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$. Taking $g=10 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}^{2}$, the maximum height reached by the stone is
(a) $30 \mathrm{~m}$
(b) $45 \mathrm{~m}$
(c) $60 \mathrm{~m}$
(d) $90 \mathrm{~m}$

\section*{EXERCISE 3.2}

(2) $2 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}^{2}$
(3) $4 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}^{2}$
(4) $20 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}^{2}$
2. The area under the velocity-time graph of a moving body gives its
(1) acceleration
(2) speed
(3) displacement
(4) jerk
